## Stories and small details

- **The first memories at Les Tourelles** date from the return to France, which was meant to be only holidays: see [01-03](01-03-early-childhood-with-family.md) for why the family stayed (Papouche and Mamouche, finances, Xavier on the way).

- **Rivalry among the cousins (draft, to complete)**: from 5 and a half to 11 Lillian lives at her grandparents' (Les Tourelles); she is one of the favourites of her grandmother and of her great-grandmother, who says she is the prettiest of all her great-grandchildren. The Parisian cousins (Kakinou's side, visiting every school holiday, about every six weeks) are jealous, as are some aunts and uncles: the grandparents were « completely focused » on Lillian, Gwen and Xavier and gave less to their own children. For the Boutroux, the children were « les scannels » (probably the family name of the father, Scannell?), the poor ones, abandoned by their father and mother. The aunts criticised Béatrice, called her a bad mother, and Lillian felt this weight and the rising resentment before each holiday. Gwen was less close to the grandmother than Lillian, who explains it by their personalities: she was calm and sensitive.

- **Aunt Kakinou (Sensitive)**: at 13, on her way to the United States for the first time, Lillian goes to Paris with Papouche and finds her aunt [Kakinou](../people/kakinou.md) completely drunk on the floor of her kitchen, rue de Courcelles. Papouche says nothing, as if it were normal. Kakinou, an alcoholic for 20 years (a bottle of gin a day, belt blows to her daughters), died in 2017; only Béatrice visited her daily and Papouche once a week. Lillian says it is where she learned to repress things. Her cousin [Gaëlle](../people/gaelle.md) later told her that her mother was ill, and that their grandmother was jealous of the attention given to Lillian and her siblings because they had no father.

- When Lillian is 11, the family moves to an apartment, and Béatrice begins to go out with men; the last one in the story is [Marc Lambert](../people/marc-lambert.md), around 1991-1992 (longer for Lillian). He takes her to boarding school one first day of term, in a leather jacket, and slips her a 50-franc note in front of everyone; he spoils all her birthdays; after the break-up he takes back the bike and the curtains and writes to Papouche to be reimbursed.
- Around 1991-1992, her American grandmother comes to Saint-Brevin for two weeks, with « Jane » (Joanne?), and stays in a hotel. She understands that Béatrice is seeing someone and tells Mark when she is back.
- Raised by her French grandparents, Papouche and Mamouche, in a reserved Saint-Brevin family: « ma chérie », four kisses, no hugs and no « je t'aime » said aloud, although she always felt the affection. A contrast with her American grandmother, the "mega hug".
- At table, Mamouche is the mistress of the house with her armchair: everyone waits for her to sit before sitting down.
- Every Christmas, Grandma Lillian (the paternal grandmother) sends the children a huge parcel of gifts from the United States, from Lillian's 6 years onward. Mark never did; Grandma earned almost nothing and bought the gifts in January, with the sales.
- Lillian keeps a slight American accent: first language English, then French.
- Around 1995, at 16, Lillian and Gwen go on holiday to New York to see their father.
- Lillian shows off her little brother Xavier to her friends, finding him super intelligent, though he is 6 years younger.
- **Gwen as a child**: lies, thefts, fights, a tomboy with no shame, hard for Lillian's gentle, « peace and joy » character; Xavier is closer to Lillian than to Gwen, who leaves them to be with her friends (see [Gwen](../people/gwen.md)).
- **Mark at a distance**: between 1995 and 1998, his only contact is phone calls, postcards, and gifts or money for birthdays. He gave Lillian a ring from his own father; about 3 years later, when Gwen was around 14, she stole it and sold it for money.
- **Lillian as the babysitter (Sensitive)**: when the family got the apartment, at 11, Lillian was already looking after Gwen (8-9) and Xavier (a toddler) while Béatrice went out, first around 19:30-20:00 to walk the dog, then more and more often at night, with several men over the following years. Béatrice was very absent. Lillian watched the two younger children, helped with the vacuum cleaner and the dishes, and was not yet paid for it (she was paid later). She says she has almost no memory of doing anything together with her mother at that age, only of her mother confiding her problems. Her grandparents Papouche and Mamouche were her anchor. The psychologist's reading, which Lillian had not seen before: Gwen has become like Béatrice and reproduces the same pattern with her children (Julia has to prepare the meals and Alexandre to look after the children), though not at the same level as Gwen herself.
- **Two phases at home**: Béatrice returned to Saint-Brevin, to Les Tourelles, with Lillian and Gwen as children and pregnant with Xavier. She lived a few years at her parents' with the whole household, where the decisions were clear and logical (Papouche and Mamouche). She then took her own apartment with her children to breathe, as a woman and a mother. With this move, Lillian became more of a friend than a daughter. She was paid a few times for babysitting, from the apartment onward, when Béatrice was not at Les Tourelles.
- **The birthday money**: Mark made a bank transfer for Lillian's birthday; Béatrice took the money and, when Lillian protested, showed her the electricity (EDF) bills, saying that the bills had to be paid. A very hard memory for a child; Lillian understands today that her mother may have had no choice.
- **Béatrice, a friend more than a mother**: Lillian says she felt she had « neither mother nor father »: Béatrice talked to her all the time about her problems, like a girlfriend, and her father was absent. It weighed on her; Gwen's problems came later. When she left at 18, she was tired of constant problems and wanted to build her own life. Her usual reaction is flight (see [Béatrice](../people/mom.md)).
- **The teacher's secret (Sensitive)**: around 11-12 (6e-5e), her close friend [Julie F.](../people/julie-flazon.md) tells her in secret that her older sister (3 years older) is in a relationship with their Spanish teacher [Mr Kérezoré](../people/kerezore.md), who was about 39-40, married with children, when the girl was 13 (he later left his wife for her). Julie carries notes between them in class. Lillian cannot keep the secret and tells [Laura Moreau](../people/laura-moreau.md), who tells Julie; Julie slaps her outside school, the only time she was attacked. They went on seeing each other. The teacher goes to Mass every Sunday, plays guitar at church and gives Spanish lessons to Mamouche, who reveres him. Lillian finds it disturbing (« chelou »), is a little afraid, does not tell her mother, and at 12 declares that she is done with church, catechism and Mass.
- **Class and social background**: Lillian says her family was not bourgeois, fatherless, « a bit of social cases »; her aunt Lalou said Béatrice was a « pute » (**Sensitive**), which Christophe attributes to jealousy. The film « La vie est un long fleuve tranquille » resonates with her.
