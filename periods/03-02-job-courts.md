## Stories and small details

- **How she got there**: after Price Chopper, Lillian looked through the Yellow Pages for schools, because she wanted to go back to school. She found Job Courts, before the hairdressing school.
- **What Job Courts is**: a fully funded residential programme, created under Bill Clinton (Lillian's words; the federal Job Corps dates from 1964, to check), for the most disadvantaged young people, from poor inner-city backgrounds. Mark had very little money, so Lillian qualified.
- **Culture shock**: one of the only white girls, among immigrants and kids from the projects, whom Lillian describes in French as « des racailles » and « des wesh ». Many were violent and had very little education. She felt « bourgeoise » in comparison.
- **Her state of mind before**: rebellious, angry at her mother, playing the "I had no dad" card, in rebellion against society like her communist father (the interviewer's framing).
- **A wake-up call**: a « social class shock ». She understood she should be grateful for what she had. Still naive and innocent at the time, she listened to them and understood them, « de loin ».
- **A Hispanic friend**: Lillian has a photo of her somewhere, perhaps at her uncle [Michael](../people/michael.md)'s.

- **Where**: Callicoon is a tiny town in upstate New York. Lillian was in Upstate New York, but was eligible although the young people came from the Bronx and Queens. It was far from Syracuse, hence full boarding.
- **Timeline (Lillian and Christophe)**: she lands in New York on 11 September 1998, then goes to Syracuse (6 to 8 months of cashier work and odd jobs), looks in the Yellow Pages « I want to change my life », finds Job Corps. Job Courts is between 1999 and 2000, before Clara's ultimatum. Clara's clash comes after.
- **Training**: she did a « front desk hospitality » training and her first university semester through Maria College.
- **Programme**: Job Corps, created under Clinton's administration to let disadvantaged youth reach studies or a vocational diploma (CAP-like). Lillian says the other students mostly wanted to validate a CV or a document, while she really wanted to train; an Omar Sy / *Intouchables* comparison from the conversation.
- **State of mind**: "oblivious, opaque": nothing reached her. She hated her mother, blamed her for everything, and saw herself as the victim, with her father as the ideal. She was so naive that she had no idea what world she was in.
- **Dormitory life**: two dormitories, one for women, with bunk beds. A roommate wore African-American braids, and Lillian learned about Black hair that evening: when the braids are removed after a month or two, the scalp has not breathed and the smell is « épouvantable »; the next day the braids were redone, even tighter.
- **Student origin**: the students came from the Bronx and Queens, not from the suburbs of Syracuse.
