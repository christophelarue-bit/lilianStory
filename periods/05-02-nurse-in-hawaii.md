## Stories and small details

- After her resignation, Lillian is called for jury duty and goes back to the United States. Her former boss near Baltimore tells her she can keep working for her as a nurse; that is where she lives with [Mohamed](../people/mohamed.md) at the time. She wants to end things with him and leave for Hawaii.
- Kim, who left the airline after her lupus diagnosis, had lived in Maui at her aunt's flat and met David there; they lived together for about 6 months.
- [Kim](../people/kim.md) suggests a flatshare at [David](../people/david-hawaii.md)'s place, her boyfriend, whom Lillian had met only briefly in 2015. Lillian organizes her move.
- At the last minute, Kim announces that she has left David and that if Lillian moves in with him, they are no longer friends. Lillian has her tickets and her suitcases ready and feels « fucked » [stuck].
- 2018: still living in Maui, working more than 40 hours a week, she flies to France in July for the family reunion at Gwen and Thomas's, is there when Papouche dies in September, then returns to Maui. She sees a psychiatrist two or three times about his death, but cannot find slots with her work and stops (**Sensitive**).
- 2019–2020: she meets [José](../people/jose.md) and leaves Maui in January 2020 to live with him in Gillette, Wyoming, where he teaches (about 5 months). She hates Gillette (rednecks, guns, oil drills, a moody boss), a shock after Maui. After one month he asks her to commit to a year; she hesitates, he refuses to renew his contract and leaves for Spain; the COVID prevents her from joining him.
- David tells her that Kim's departure changes nothing and that she can still come. He hardly knows her, she is the girlfriend of his ex who has just left him, and yet he welcomes her. It allows her to live her experience in Maui.

- **The islands**: Lillian went to Kauai, Oahu (Honolulu), Maui and « Lahaina » (a town on Maui; the one-hour boat trip from there probably goes to another island, Lanai or Molokai, to confirm), several times for the boat. Of the 8 Hawaiian islands, two are reserves closed to tourists. Maui, where she lived, looks like a man in profile (head and bust). The Big Island is not her place.
- **Kauai**: reached in about 40 minutes by plane. With a pilot, she did a tour in a tiny plane, stayed in a camper van and hiked in a canyon that looks like a small Grand Canyon (Waimea?); some Jurassic Park scenes were shot there. At the time Kauai was much less commercial than Maui: they camped by the sea and were alone, a magical experience.
- **David's house, the first year**: party after party, always people and music. David is very extroverted; Lillian is more introverted and after a while wanted her own space.
- **The yurt in the jungle, Haiku (3 to 4 months)**: a dream of hers was to sleep in the jungle. She found an ad on Craigslist: a surfer owned a huge plot with 8 to 10 yurts. He warned her about the rain and humidity, she insisted. 40 minutes by car, on a dirt road without lights, so she stopped going out for sunsets. She had two kittens there. Outdoor shower and toilet for two yurts, with a gas bottle for hot water, changed in turn, and she often forgot, so no hot water; in heavy rain she did not go out and used a bowl. Mould grew on her things and a doctor found fungus on her abdomen and told her to move.
- **Two housemates (Kisha and another woman)**: women her age, one a friend of David's, into spirituality, a bit of a guru. The deal was 1,000 dollars for a room in a 3-bedroom house, then 1,200, plus 200 dollars added for cable TV that Lillian did not watch. Lillian worked varying shifts (14h to 23h, sometimes 23h to 7h or from 16h); the garage had one space, and Kisha woke her two hours after she parked in front of it to move the car. Lillian felt cheated and the vibe was wrong. After one or two months she gave one month's notice; her deposit took months to be returned although she damaged nothing.
- **Back at David's**: she rents a room there for about 600 to 800 dollars a month, less than the 1,000 elsewhere, with a better vibe. Rent in the United States is very expensive: an apartment would have been more than 2,000 dollars.
